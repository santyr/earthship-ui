# Predominant daily weather icons — October 20

The owner's post-audit refinement asks Home and Weather to summarize the predominant daily conditions, including mostly sunny days. The earlier rule let a daily total of 1 mm override the sky icon regardless of timing. A retained live example had about 46% mean daytime cloud cover and only one materially wet daylight period, but displayed rain. This is a display-policy correction; quantitative predictions, source codes, probabilities, model identities and published originals retain their meanings.

The shared `forecastCondition` daily path now classifies available forecast daylight periods. Cloud cover below 25% is sunny, 25% through below 75% is partly cloudy, and 75% or more is cloudy. Material precipitation requires at least 1 mm across daylight periods; an individual wet period contributes at least 0.1 mm. The category occupying more than half the periods wins. Thus mostly sunny and mostly partly cloudy days retain their sky icon despite brief or overnight rain. A tie/mixed sky uses partly cloudy, or a combined sky/precipitation icon when no category has a majority and at least a quarter of periods are materially wet. These are explicit display heuristics, not probabilities or qualified skill thresholds.

The daily row and detail dialog use the same function. Original precipitation amounts remain visible. Actual source weather codes retain precedence, and the hourly strip retains its existing per-period rule. No snow, thunderstorm, precipitation probability or AQI is inferred from unsupported Google fields.

Unknown daylight, missing precipitation or interior timing gaps keep a timing-unavailable qualifier. The existing summary is a fallback when hourly cloud detail is absent. Declared remaining-day precipitation uses only endpoints after `precipitationFromAt` through `precipitationThroughAt`; complete one-, two- and three-period remainders can still be classified. This repaired an independently reproduced late-afternoon edge case. The icon describes available forecast daylight; it does not manufacture missing coverage.
